# FAQ copy: term introduction and dependency order

The visible learning sequence remains predict, feedback, watch one move, hold, next. Optional depth remains collapsed. No extra panels or long article are added to the player.

| Term | First definition | Used to explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piston | Intro: sliding plug | Changing the space that holds gas |
| Cylinder | Intro: space around that plug | Intake, squeezing, gas push |
| Connecting rod | Intro: jointed link | Straight motion to crank motion |
| Crankshaft | Intro: turning shaft | Rotation |
| Inlet valve | Intake: small entry door | Trapping air on the following move |
| Stroke | Collapsed intake depth: one trip up/down | Four-stroke cycle, optional depth |
| Compression | Squeeze: trapping and squeezing air | Why air gets hot before fuel enters |
| Injector | Power: device that sprays fuel | Why fuel meets the hot air at the right time |
| Power stroke | Power: hot gas expands and pushes down | The single burning-energy input |
| Exhaust valve | Exhaust: outlet door | Clearing gas for the next intake |
| Ignition / combustion | Collapsed power depth: starting to burn / burning | Technical names after the mechanism |
| Crown / wrist pin | Optional piston FAQ: top / joint pin | Deeper part explanation only |
| Flywheel | Optional crankshaft FAQ: attached disc | Motion carried through the other strokes |

Why-hook order: why a moving plug and jointed link, why air first, why trap and squeeze it, why fuel enters hot air, why clear used gas, why a leak undermines compression, how the chain produces rotation. The transfer question still checks the air-trapping dependency. Wrong answers retain mechanism-specific explanations rather than scores.

Source checked 10 November 2026: US Department of Energy, Internal Combustion Engine Basics, https://www.energy.gov/cmei/vehicles/articles/internal-combustion-engine-basics . Schematic timing/shape limits remain disclosed. No real efficiency, pressure or temperature claim.
